## Initial Thesis

McDonald's reported 2025 Systemwide sales of $139.4 billion, a 46.1% operating margin, and $10.6 billion of cash provided by operations. [10-K] Its predominantly franchised model allows the company to participate in a Systemwide sales base that is larger than revenue recognized directly on its income statement. [10-K]

Those results are evidence of a high-quality and cash-generative business. [Judgment] They do not, by themselves, establish that MCD was attractively valued on September 1, 2026. [Judgment] Reported long-term debt was $39.973 billion at December 31, 2025, so the enterprise-to-equity bridge is consequential to any estimate of common equity value. [10-K; Judgment] I have not yet estimated operating value, established a valuation-date diluted share count, or compared an implied per-share value with the market price on the valuation date. [Research plan]

## Consequential Assumptions

The following are assumptions to test, rather than established facts.

1. McDonald's predominantly franchised model will continue to generate attractive margins and cash flows from its Systemwide sales base. [Judgment] This matters because weaker franchisee profitability, higher restaurant costs, weaker traffic, or changes in contract economics could reduce operating value even if restaurant count grows. [Judgment]
2. Systemwide sales are a useful operating-value driver for understanding the scale and direction of McDonald's underlying restaurant system. [Judgment] This matters only if the relationship between Systemwide sales and cash flows available to McDonald's Corporation remains economically meaningful; Systemwide sales are not the same as corporate revenue. [Judgment]
3. The 46.1% 2025 operating margin is useful evidence of current earnings power, but it should not be assumed to remain constant indefinitely. [10-K; Judgment] Small changes in sustainable margin could materially change normalized operating profit, free cash flow, and enterprise value. [Judgment]
4. Some portion of the $774 million cash balance may be non-operating cash when reconciling enterprise value to equity value. [10-K; Judgment] Treating all cash as excess when some is required for normal operations would overstate common equity value. [Judgment]
5. Debt and other non-common-equity claims must be identified consistently and deducted from enterprise value. [Judgment] Because McDonald's reported a large debt balance, classification or sign errors could materially distort estimated common equity value. [10-K; Judgment]
6. The 716.4 million 2025 weighted-average diluted shares are an initial dilution reference, but a more current valuation-date share count is needed before completing a final per-share valuation. [10-K; Judgment] Repurchases, option exercises, equity awards, or other changes in shares outstanding can alter per-share value even when total common equity value is unchanged. [Judgment]

## Unknowns

The following remain unknown and are not assumed in this edition. [Research plan]

1. MCD's market price on September 1, 2026.
2. The appropriate normalized operating-profit or free-cash-flow base.
3. The sustainable long-term Systemwide sales growth rate.
4. The sustainable operating margin rather than a mechanical use of the 46.1% 2025 margin.
5. The reinvestment level required to support future growth.
6. The amount of the $774 million cash balance that is non-operating or excess cash.
7. The debt and debt-like claims for the final enterprise-to-equity bridge.
8. Whether material noncontrolling interests, pension deficits, leases, or other claims need separate treatment.
9. The diluted share count that best represents September 1, 2026 rather than the 2025 weighted-average diluted count.
10. The appropriate discount rate or WACC for McDonald's operating and capital-structure risk.
11. An economically supportable terminal growth rate or exit assumption.
12. The sensitivity of estimated per-share value to growth, margins, WACC, and terminal assumptions.

## Research Plan

1. Review McDonald's filings issued after the 2025 10-K and before September 1, 2026 to update cash, debt, shares outstanding, diluted-share evidence, operating performance, and material capital-allocation changes. [Research plan; [course source guide](sources.md)]
2. Analyze revenue, Systemwide sales, operating income, operating margin, cash from operations, capital expenditures, and free cash flow, separating recurring economics from unusual or nonrecurring items. [Research plan]
3. Forecast Systemwide sales growth, corporate revenue growth, operating margin, taxes, depreciation and amortization, capital expenditures, working capital, and free cash flow. [Research plan]
4. Estimate enterprise value with a supportable framework such as a DCF based on normalized free cash flow, including forecast FCFF, WACC, terminal value, and present value of operating assets. [Research plan]
5. Reconcile enterprise value to common equity value by adding qualifying non-operating cash or assets and subtracting debt, other debt-like claims, noncontrolling interests if applicable, and other relevant claims. [Research plan]
6. Establish a valuation-date diluted share count by reconciling common shares outstanding, share-based compensation, options or awards, treasury-stock-method dilution where appropriate, and repurchase activity. [Research plan]
7. Divide common equity value by diluted shares to estimate intrinsic value per share, then compare it with the September 1, 2026 market price and assess margin of safety. [Research plan]
8. Compare the current filing with the two preceding MCD 10-Ks, then review dated company news releases and a CEO interview or earnings-call source to test whether management's stated expectations were realized. [Research plan; [course source guide](sources.md)]
9. Test lower Systemwide sales growth, lower margins, higher WACC, different terminal growth, higher reinvestment, different cash and debt treatment, and changes in diluted shares to identify evidence that would invalidate the thesis. [Research plan]

| Enterprise value | $1,000 | [Course case] |

| Noncontrolling interest | $20 | [Course case] |

| Diluted shares | 100 | [Course case] |

### Calculation A — NOPAT

NOPAT = EBIT × (1 - tax rate) = 150 × (1 - 0.25) = **$112.5 million**. [Course case]

### Calculation B — FCFF

FCFF = NOPAT + D&A - Capital Expenditures - Increase in Operating NWC = 112.5 + 40 - 60 - 15 = **$77.5 million**. [Course case]

### Calculation C — Common Equity Value

Common Equity Value = Enterprise Value + Non-operating Cash - Debt - Noncontrolling Interest - Unfunded Pension = 1,000 + 100 - 250 - 20 - 10 = **$820 million**. [Course case]

### Calculation D — Per-Share Value

Per-share value = Common Equity Value / Diluted Shares = $820 million / 100 million shares = **$8.20 per share**. [Course case]

### Reconciliation and Sign-Error Check

The synthetic case produces NOPAT of $112.5M, FCFF of $77.5M, common equity value of $820M, and per-share value of $8.20. [Course case]

Debt is subtracted when converting enterprise value to common equity value. [Course case] If the $250 million debt were incorrectly added, common equity value would be $1,320 million and per-share value would be $13.20. [Course case] That sign error would overstate equity value by $500 million, or $5.00 per share, relative to the correct $8.20 result. [Course case]
